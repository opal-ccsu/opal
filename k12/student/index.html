The map about 2 minutes

Career ready means two things.

You can do the work. And you can say what you can do.

There are four parts. These are the real names. You'll see them again in high school, in college, and on job applications.

Experiential LearningLearning by doing. Sports, band, clubs, a job, babysitting, fixing something, a game you got good at.
Career MentorshipPeople who help you figure things out. A coach, a teacher, a relative, a boss, an older friend.
Core CompetenciesSkills every job needs. Talking clearly, working with people, solving problems, showing up.
Career-Specific KSAsWhat you Know comes first. Then what you can do (Skills) and what you're good at (Abilities), for one kind of work.

One more thing, and a lot of adults get this wrong. A skill is made of things you know. "Good at teamwork" is just a label until you can say what you knew that made the team work. So every time you name a skill here, you'll also name what you knew.

Now let's find all four in your life.

Something you did about 3 minutes Experiential Learning

Think of one thing you did this year that was not a test or a worksheet.

Big or small. In school or out. Need ideas? Tap one.

Write something first.
Try: "I learned to run the mower at my uncle's." "I got past a boss in a game by figuring out its pattern." "I ran the sound board for the school play."

Mistakes count. That's where the learning is.

Who helped about 2 minutes Career Mentorship

Who helped you with that?

Or showed you something you didn't know?

Name one person first. If nobody did, write "nobody." That counts.
Try: "My coach showed me how to keep score." "My school counselor helped me pick classes." "My cousin taught me to change a tire." "Nobody. I figured it out alone." (That counts. Write it.)
Try: "How did you learn that?" "What would you do differently?" "What do you wish you knew at my age?"

You can only ask a good question about something you already know a little about. That's how knowing grows.

What it took about 3 minutes Core Competencies

Employers say every job needs these eight skills.

Tap up to three you used.

You already picked three. Tap one to unpick it, then pick your new one.
Tap at least one skill.
Name one thing you had to know.
Try: "I had to know the rules of the game." "I had to know how the register worked." "I had to know who was in charge and what they wanted."

That's the part that makes it real. Anyone can say "teamwork." Not everyone can say what they knew. Real names on the right; you'll see those words on job postings.

Where it could go about 2 minutes Career-Specific KSAs

Name one kind of work you're curious about.

It doesn't have to be forever.

Write something first. A guess is fine.
Try: "A nurse has to know how medicines mix." "A mechanic has to know how an engine cycles." "A game designer has to know how to make a level hard but fair."

Know comes first. The doing is built on it. That's a KSA, and you'll collect more every year.

Say it about 2 minutes

Last one.

If a relative asked, "What are you learning in school right now?" what would you say?

0 words. Aim for at least 25.
Aim for at least 25 words. Two or three sentences is fine.
Try: "I'm learning how ___ works." "I know now that ___, which I didn't before." "The hardest thing I've learned this year is ___."

Name something you know now that you didn't before. Not "stuff." The actual thing. This is your before. Next time you'll answer again, without looking, and see what changed.
